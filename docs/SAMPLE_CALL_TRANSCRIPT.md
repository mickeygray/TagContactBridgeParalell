# Sample call transcript — WYNN tax-resolution (full arc)

A complete, realistic outbound tax-resolution sales call: intro → discovery → expert framing → pitch → fee → objections (price, trust, stall) → conditional close. Used as the canonical input for the full-call coaching demo. Agent = Marcus (Wynn Tax), Prospect = Linda (inquired online).

---

**Agent:** Hi, is this Linda? This is Marcus calling from Wynn Tax Solutions — you'd reached out about some help with the IRS. Did I catch you at an okay time?

**Prospect:** Uh, yeah, this is Linda. I did fill something out online a while back. What's this about exactly?

**Agent:** Of course — you'd looked into getting help with a tax balance. I just want to ask a few quick questions to see where things stand and whether we can actually help. Sound fair?

**Prospect:** Okay, sure.

**Agent:** Great. So roughly, do you know about how much you owe the IRS right now?

**Prospect:** It's federal. Somewhere around thirty-two thousand, I think? Maybe a little more with the interest.

**Agent:** Okay, thirty-two and climbing. And are you caught up on filing your returns, or are there any years that didn't get filed?

**Prospect:** That's part of the problem. I didn't file 2021 or 2022. Things got messy after my divorce.

**Agent:** Understood, that happens more than you'd think. And have they started any collection action — are you getting letters, or is anything being taken out of your pay or your bank account?

**Prospect:** They're garnishing my wages now. That's actually why I'm finally dealing with it. It started last month and it's killing me.

**Agent:** Okay, so there's an active garnishment — that's the urgent piece. Quick one: are you a W-2 employee, or self-employed?

**Prospect:** W-2. I work at a warehouse.

**Agent:** Got it. And just so I understand the situation — is paying the whole thing off at once realistic, or is money pretty tight right now?

**Prospect:** It's tight. Real tight, especially with them taking part of my check. I can't just write a check for thirty grand.

**Agent:** That's exactly the situation we handle. Let me explain what we're looking at. Three things matter to the IRS: what you owe, what's filed, and where you stand on their radar. Right now you've got a balance, two unfiled years, and active enforcement — that combination is what's driving the garnishment.

**Prospect:** Okay.

**Agent:** Here's the good news: that garnishment isn't permanent, and the unfiled returns are fixable. But the IRS won't negotiate anything with you while you're not in compliance — the missing returns have to get filed first. That's step one.

**Prospect:** So how does that actually work? Like what do you guys do?

**Agent:** Great question. The first thing we do is get you represented — we file a Power of Attorney, Form 2848, so we deal with the IRS directly. From that point you stop fielding their calls and letters; they come to us. Then we pull your full transcripts, get those two returns filed, and from there we negotiate the actual resolution — whether that's an installment agreement, a hardship status, or reducing penalties.

**Prospect:** And that stops the garnishment?

**Agent:** Once we're representing you and moving toward compliance, getting that garnishment released is one of the first things we push for. I can't promise a date, but it's a priority because it's bleeding you every paycheck.

**Prospect:** Okay. So what does something like this cost?

**Agent:** For your situation — the representation, the two returns, and the resolution work — the flat legal fee is eighteen hundred dollars.

**Prospect:** Eighteen hundred? That's a lot. I can't pay that all at once, not with them already taking my money.

**Agent:** I hear you, and that's a fair concern. Let me ask — is it that the eighteen hundred just isn't there right now, or are you weighing whether it's worth it?

**Prospect:** I mean... both, honestly. It's a lot of money and I don't even know if it'll work.

**Agent:** Both fair. On whether it works — the garnishment's taking what, a few hundred a paycheck? Over a year that's more than the fee, and it doesn't fix anything; it just keeps bleeding. The fee is fixed, and it's the thing that actually stops the bleeding. On the money being tight — we don't have to do it all at once. We can start with the first phase, get the POA on file and the garnishment release in motion, and structure the rest. Would splitting it into a couple of payments make this doable?

**Prospect:** Maybe. How would that work?

**Agent:** Typically we'd take a portion today to file the Power of Attorney and get started, then the balance over the next couple of months. The important thing is getting that POA filed so we can step in front of the IRS for you. Can I get a few details to set that up?

**Prospect:** Okay, hold on though. I've gotten calls from these tax companies before and honestly some of them felt like a scam. How do I know you're legit?

**Agent:** You're right to ask — this industry's earned that skepticism. Couple of things: you can pull up our site right now while we're on the phone and look us up. And notice I'm not promising you we'll settle for pennies on the dollar — anybody who promises you a specific number before they've seen your IRS file is the one you should worry about. What I'm telling you is the process: POA, transcripts, file the returns, then negotiate from real numbers.

**Prospect:** Okay. That actually makes sense. Let me think about it for a day and I'll call you back.

**Agent:** I understand it's a lot to take in. The only thing is — that garnishment doesn't pause while you think it over, and those unfiled years keep aging. Usually when someone wants to think about it, it's one of two things: the money, or whether this really works. Which one's the real question for you?

**Prospect:** I guess... the money. Whether I can swing even the first payment.

**Agent:** Okay, that we can work with. If we can get the first payment to a number that works for you today, are you ready to get started and get that garnishment release moving?

**Prospect:** ...Yeah. Yeah, okay. If we can make the first part manageable, let's do it.

**Agent:** Perfect. Let me get a few things to file your Power of Attorney and get you on record as represented. First, can I confirm your full legal name and the best email for you?
