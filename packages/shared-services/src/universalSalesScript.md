Section 2: Tax Problem
Transition Statement: "Great, let me ask a few questions so I can understand your situation better."
🟩 Topic: Amount Owed
✅ Questions to Ask
📌 Follow-Up Based on Answers
“Do you know how much you owe the IRS or State right now?”
If they know the balance:“Great — that helps us know what kind of programs we can target for you. Depending on income and hardship, we may be able to reduce it or stop collection efforts.”
“Is that amount just federal, or does it include state as well?”
If they don’t know:“No problem — we pull your IRS transcripts directly once we file Power of Attorney. That gives us the full picture — what’s owed, from when, and how it’s growing.”
“Has that balance gone up recently?”

🟩 Topic: Years Unfiled
✅ Questions to Ask
📌 Follow-Up Based on Answers
“Do you have any years you haven’t filed yet?”
If they have unfiled years:“We can help you get those filed fast. Filing gets you back in good standing and stops the IRS from filing substitute returns, which usually overstate what you owe.”
“Roughly how many years are unfiled?”
If everything is filed:“Good — that makes the resolution process easier. We’ll still double-check transcripts to confirm the IRS has everything.”
“Have you received any letters about missing returns?”

🟩 Topic: Collection Activity (Levy, Garnishment, Lien)
✅ Questions to Ask
📌 Follow-Up Based on Answers
“Are you currently being garnished or levied?”
If there’s active garnishment/levy:“We can often stop garnishments or bank levies once we file Power of Attorney — sometimes same-day. That’s priority one.”
“Have you seen anything about a tax lien?”
If there’s a lien:“A lien is serious — it means the IRS sees the balance as collectible. We’ll need to act quickly to avoid escalation.”
“When was the last time the IRS or State contacted you?”
If there’s no activity (yet):“That’s actually the best time to step in — before they start enforcement. Once they do, it’s harder and more expensive to fix.”
🟩 Topic: Previous Resolution Attempts
✅ Questions to Ask
📌 Follow-Up Based on Answers
“Have you ever worked with a tax firm or accountant to resolve this?”
If they worked with someone before:“A lot of clients come to us after bad experiences. We do everything in writing, and show you the case file directly from the IRS so you know where you stand.”
“Did they finish the case? Were you satisfied?”
If they haven’t tried yet:“Perfect — that gives us a clean slate to work from. We’ll take care of the research and build a plan based on facts, not guesses.”
“Have you been on a payment plan before?”

Section 3: Financial Information
Transition Statement: "Thanks, now I just need to gather your financial snapshot so we know what resolution options apply."
🟩 Topic: Employment / Income
✅ Questions to Ask
📌 Follow-Up Based on Answers
“What kind of work do you do?”
If employed with consistent income:“That helps — your income determines which IRS programs you may qualify for, like hardship status or partial payment plans.”
“How long have you been doing that?”
If self-employed or inconsistent income:“We specialize in complex cases like that. We can document fluctuations to your benefit in the resolution phase.”
“What’s your monthly or yearly income — roughly?”

🟩 Topic: Major Expenses
✅ Questions to Ask
📌 Follow-Up Based on Answers
“What are your biggest monthly expenses — rent, car payments, childcare?”
If they have high expenses:“That can work in your favor — the IRS has to consider living expenses when determining what you can realistically afford.”
“Do you support anyone else in the household?”
If expenses are low or unclear:“We’ll use standard allowable expenses if needed, but getting exact numbers may open up more options.”
“Are there any unusual or medical expenses we should consider?”

🟩 Topic: Assets (Bank, Retirement, Property)
✅ Questions to Ask
📌 Follow-Up Based on Answers
“Do you have any bank accounts with more than $1,000?”
If they have significant assets:“Good to know — we’ll factor that into the strategy. Certain programs require us to explain how those assets are used or protected.”
“Any retirement accounts — 401k, IRA, pension?”
If assets are minimal:“That can actually help — the less the IRS thinks they can collect, the more options you may have for settlement or hardship relief.”
“Do you own a home or any other property?”

🟩 Topic: Payment Method
✅ Questions to Ask
📌 Follow-Up Based on Answers
“Will you be using a debit or credit card today to secure representation?”
If ready to pay:“Perfect. Once I have that, we’ll send out the Power of Attorney and start protecting you immediately.”
“Is that under your name or someone else’s?”
If hesitant:“This payment is for Phase 1 only — investigation and protection. You’re not committing to anything beyond that today.”
“Do you have that handy now?”

🟩 Topic: Social Security Number (SSN)
✅ Questions to Ask
📌 Follow-Up Based on Answers
“Can I get your Social Security Number so we can complete the Power of Attorney?”
If willing to provide SSN:“Perfect. This allows us to pull your full IRS file and speak to them directly on your behalf. Without it, we can’t legally access or protect your case.”
“Is this the same SSN tied to your IRS records?”
If hesitant:“I totally understand — just so you know, this is required for us to legally represent you with the IRS. We’re a licensed firm and your info is secure. Nothing moves forward without this.”
“Have you used this number consistently with past returns?”

Closing Statement: "Thanks for your patience. Based on what you told me, here’s what we can do to get you started today."
🟩 Services Rendered Today (Explained with Examples)
Power of Attorney (POA):
“This gives us the legal right to talk to the IRS or State on your behalf.”
Example 1: “Instead of them calling or sending letters to you, they now have to speak to us directly.”
Example 2: “If you’re being garnished or levied, we can call and request a hold the same day the POA is filed.”
Tax Filing (Up to 2 Years):
“We’ll file missing returns so you’re back in good standing and protected from penalties.”
Example 1: “If you haven’t filed 2021 or 2022, we get those done immediately to avoid substitute returns.”
Example 2: “Even if you don’t have all your documents, we can pull your wage and income reports directly from the IRS.”
Transcript Analysis:
“We pull and review your complete IRS file to confirm what’s really going on.”
Example 1: “We’ll verify your total balance and see which years the IRS considers unresolved or collectible.”
Example 2: “We check for penalties, interest, and old years that may be close to expiration.”
Resolution Roadmap:
“Based on your financials, we map out your options — from settlement to hardship protection.”
Example 1: “If you qualify, we can pursue an Offer in Compromise to reduce what you owe.”
Example 2: “If your income is low or unstable, we can request Currently Not Collectible status to stop all collection efforts.”
